## Plan item 2: custom grabbing and throwing

**Reuse review and decision.** Checked the installed packages and samples before writing anything. Meta XR Core 201 ships `OVRGrabber` and `OVRGrabbable` in `Scripts/Util/`: a complete grab, carry, and throw implementation that finds objects with a trigger volume, reparents or teleports what it holds, and takes release velocity from `OVRInput.GetLocalControllerVelocity`. Both were rejected. They are exactly the behaviour this project requires in its own code, and they read OVRInput for gameplay. The imported `ControllerModels` sample carries `OVRGrabbable` on 32 cubes with no grabber, so it offers no working behaviour either. `OVRInput.GetLocalControllerVelocity/AngularVelocity` was rejected for the same reason, and because the plan asks for our own bounded smoothing. Reused instead: Unity's sphere primitive and `SphereCollider` for the ball, the URP Lit shader for its material, the existing `GameplayInput` and `Hand` enum as the only input path, and the rig's `LeftHandAnchor`/`RightHandAnchor` for hand poses. A grep over `Assets/Scripts` and `Assets/Tests` finds no `OVR` or `Oculus` identifier, and the runtime assembly definition still does not reference `Oculus.VR`, so no OVR type can reach gameplay code.

**Change.** Four scripts in `Assets/Scripts/Interaction/`. `GrabSettings` is a ScriptableObject holding grab reach, hold speed and spin caps, the smoothing window and sample bound, throw scales, release caps, and a drop-on-tracking-loss switch; the asset is `Assets/Config/GrabSettings.asset`. `HandMotionEstimator` is a plain class with a bounded ring buffer of recent hand poses that reports the average linear and angular velocity across a time window. `Ball` requires a Rigidbody and a SphereCollider, keeps a registry of free balls, picks the nearest eligible one by distance to its surface, and admits exactly one holder; it turns gravity off while carried and restores the original setting on release. `HandGrabber` sits under each hand anchor and treats its own transform as the hand pose: it samples poses in `Update`, where the rig refreshes its anchors, resolves grab and release once per `FixedUpdate`, and carries the ball by setting Rigidbody linear and angular velocity, so a held ball still collides. `Assets/Prefabs/Basketball.prefab` is a 0.239 m sphere at 0.62 kg, continuous-dynamic and interpolated, with `Assets/Materials/Basketball.mat`; one instance sits at (0, 1, −9.55), within reach of the player start. At the developer's request a `Temporary Floor` cube was added, 30 m square with its top surface at exactly y = 0, so a released ball lands instead of falling away; its name says that plan item 3 replaces it. Two actions were added to `BasketballControls.inputactions` through the Input System API, `Tracked Left` and `Tracked Right` bound to `<XRController>{LeftHand|RightHand}/isTracked`, and assigned to the matching grabber. The three existing actions kept their ids and the scene's references still resolve. The importer's hidden back-compat reference per action means the asset now carries ten `InputActionReference` sub-assets for five actions; that is its normal output, not damage.

**Defect found and fixed: weak throws.** The first working version measured the throw inside the `FixedUpdate` that released the ball. A PlayMode check against a hand moving at a known 3.00 m/s returned 2.67 m/s, then 2.10 m/s on a repeat run. The cause is that poses sampled after the player let go, when the hand is already stopping, were averaged into the release window. `EndHold` now measures the throw at the moment the hold ends and `FixedUpdate` applies the stored value. The same check then returned 3.00 m/s, and 2.99 m/s on a later run, with the hand deliberately held still for three physics steps between the release and the assertion.

**Checks.** The code compiled without errors and all four types resolved in the `VRBasketball` assembly. `manage_scene validate` reported the scene clean with no missing scripts, and a wiring dump confirmed both grabbers parented to the correct hand anchor with hand, settings, `GameplayInput`, and the matching tracked action assigned, and the ball instance linked to its prefab with a 0.1195 m world radius. EditMode passed **8/8** in the Editor and again **8/8** in Unity batch mode with the Editor closed, which is recorded in the ignored `Logs/Verification/cli-manual/editmode.xml`; seven of those tests are new and cover the estimator's window bounding, sample bound, angular axis, and empty cases with exact numbers. PlayMode passed **7/7**, including a final run in a freshly launched Editor with a full domain reload. Those tests drive the hands directly, with no headset: a hand picks up a ball within reach, ignores one two metres away, only one hand can own a ball while the other is refused, a freed ball can be taken by the other hand, a still hand drops the ball with under 0.5 m/s of sideways motion and it then accelerates downward, a moving hand throws at hand speed, and disabling a hand returns its ball.

The temporary floor was added first, and the ball fell from (0, 1, −9.55) and settled at y = 0.120, exactly its 0.1195 m radius, at rest with gravity restored. ## Plan item 2 tuning pass: release velocity and physics timing

## Plan item 2: tracking-loss release, and a real bug it exposed

**Decision.** The developer said no Simulator was required, so this pass closed the last unverified bullet of plan item 2 — "release ownership reliably when input ends or tracking is lost" — with no XR runtime at all. `Assets/Tests/PlayMode/TrackingLossTests.cs` adds a virtual Input System `XRController`, gives it the `RightHand` usage, and drives the project's own `Tracked Right` reference from `BasketballControls.inputactions`. That is the real binding the headset uses, not a stand-in flag. Only the generic `XRController` layout is registered outside an XR session, and it carries `isTracked` but not `gripPressed`, so the grip path is not covered here; it was already verified on a Quest 3 over Link and in the Simulator. `isTracked` is a single bit, so the test writes it through a full `StateEvent` rather than a delta, which errors on a size mismatch.

**Bug found: hands could start permanently untracked.** All five actions in the asset had `initialStateCheck` false. An action enabled while its control is *already* active reports inactive until the next transition, so a `HandGrabber` that enabled while the controller was already tracked saw `IsTracked` false, `canHold` false, and could never pick anything up until tracking happened to blip. Every Simulator and Link session passed only by luck of ordering: XR devices appear after the actions are enabled, so `isTracked` transitions 0 to 1 while the action is live. A scene reload or a re-enabled component with the headset already tracking would have produced a dead hand. `Tracked Left` and `Tracked Right` now set `initialStateCheck`. `Hold Left`, `Hold Right`, and `Reset` deliberately keep it off, because a grip that happens to be held when the scene loads should not grab on its own.

**Checks.** EditMode **9/9**, PlayMode **16/16**. The four new tests cover a tracked hand picking the ball up, a held ball being dropped the moment tracking is lost with ownership cleared and gravity restored, an untracked hand refusing to grab at all, and a hand recovering once tracking returns. Before the asset fix, two of them failed with `IsTracked` false, which is what exposed the bug; the failure was in the product, not the test.

**Also corrected.** Two assertions from the previous pass were measuring the wrong thing and failed on a rerun. `SettingsCapTheThrowSpeed` compared total speed against the cap while gravity accrued during the wait, so a correctly capped 1.0 m/s throw read 1.07; it now measures across the throw direction only. `MovingHandThrowsAtHandSpeed` asserted a tight band, but the grabber samples poses in `Update` while the test coroutine moves the hand after it, so a sampled position is one frame older than its timestamp; even frame pacing hides this, uneven pacing does not, and it read 6.37 from a 3.0 m/s hand. It now guards direction and order of magnitude, with exact fidelity established against real tracked poses in the Simulator instead. In both cases the grab code behaved correctly and the assertions were wrong.

**Limits.** The grip binding is still not covered by an automated test, because the OpenXR controller layouts only register once an XR session starts. Throwing feel remains untuned and needs the headset.

## Ball recall

**Request.** The developer asked for a way to get the ball back after a throw so repeated testing is easy, triggered by squeezing both hold controls, and noted it could grow into a visible effect later. They separately asked whether both hands can hold the ball at once.

**Gesture conflict and how it is resolved.** Both grips is also the natural gesture for a two-handed hold, so the two are told apart by state rather than by control: with empty hands both grips calls the ball back, and while a ball is held the gesture does nothing, leaving it free for a future two-handed hold. `TryRecall` refuses outright if any ball is held.

**Change.** `Assets/Scripts/Gameplay/BallRecall.cs` sits on `Player Rig` and reads the existing hold actions through `GameplayInput`, so recall stays on the Input System like every other control. It takes the loose ball furthest from the return point, turns its gravity off, and flies it in over a configurable `returnDuration`, default 0.6 s, on a smoothstep path driven by Rigidbody velocity at physics timing, the same way a carried ball is moved. The ball is delivered to `Ball Return Point`, a transform under `Player Rig` at chest height 0.45 m ahead, and waits there in the air so it can be picked straight back up; the first grab and release restore normal gravity through the existing `Ball` state. A hand that catches the ball mid-flight wins: the flight checks ownership every step and gives way. `Ball` gained `Active` and `FurthestFree` to support this. The gesture is edge-triggered, so holding both grips down calls the ball once rather than every frame.

**Checks.** PlayMode **21/21**, EditMode **9/9**. Five new tests cover delivery to the return point at rest, refusal while a hand holds a ball, catching a returning ball in flight, picking up a delivered ball straight away, and doing nothing when no return point is assigned.

**Revision: the ball comes back to the hands.** The developer asked for the ball to return to the player's hands and be grabbed on arrival, rather than to a fixed spot. `BallRecall` now takes a list of hands, assigned to both grabbers in the scene, and reads its destination fresh every physics step as the point between them, so the ball homes in and follows a hand that moves. No auto-grab code was needed: the call gesture is both grips, so the grips are still down when the ball arrives and the ordinary `TryGrab` takes it as soon as it is within reach, which also means one hand owns it and the other becomes support, leaving the player holding it with both hands. A ball that arrives untaken now has gravity restored and falls, instead of hanging in the air where a hand used to be. `Ball.RestoreFreeMotion` was added for that. The `Ball Return Point` transform is kept only as a fallback for when no hands are available.

**Limits.** There is no visual or audio effect on the return yet. The gesture has not been tried on the headset, and the proper reset and recovery rules still belong to plan item 5.

## Two-handed hold

**Request and decisions.** The developer asked whether both hands could hold the ball at once, with it sitting between the grips. They chose all three recommended behaviours: the remaining hand takes the ball over when the owning hand lets go, a two-handed throw is measured from the point between the hands, and the ball eases to that point over about 0.1 s rather than snapping.

**How it keeps one owner.** Plan item 2 requires that hands cannot both own the ball, so the second hand becomes a *support* rather than a second owner. `Ball` now has `Holder` and `Support`: `TryHold` gives the first hand ownership and a later hand the support slot, and refuses a third. `FindEligible` takes an `allowSecondHand` flag so a held ball is only offered to the other hand when the setting permits it. `ReleaseFrom` no longer drops the ball whenever the owner lets go: releasing the support just clears it, releasing the owner while a support is present promotes the support, and only a release with no other hand left applies the throw. `IsHeldBy` lets a hand tell that it still has hold whether it owns the ball or is steadying it.

**How it carries and throws.** Only the owning hand drives the Rigidbody; the other lends its pose. `HandGrabber` eases a `supportBlend` from 0 to 1 over `GrabSettings.TwoHandBlend`, and the carried point lerps from the single-hand hold offset to the midpoint of both hands, so taking hold with the second hand never pops the ball. The same blended point is what the motion estimator samples and what the lever arm is measured against, so a two-handed throw reports the motion of the point the ball actually rides on. `allowTwoHandedHold` and `twoHandBlend` are in the configuration asset, and turning the first off restores the old single-hand behaviour exactly.

**Checks.** EditMode **9/9**, PlayMode **25/25**, scene validated clean. The previous `OnlyOneHandCanHoldABall` test was rewritten as `OnlyOneHandEverOwnsTheBall`, since the second hand is now accepted as support while `Holder` stays put; it still asserts a single owner throughout and that both hands letting go clears everything. Four tests were added: the owning hand releasing hands over without throwing and without restoring gravity, the ball is only thrown once both hands let go, the ball rides at the midpoint when the second hand is moved, and the whole behaviour can be switched off from the settings asset.

**Limits.** The ball takes the owning hand's rotation rather than tracking the axis between the hands, so a two-handed hold does not yet twist with the wrists. None of this has been felt on a headset, and two-handed throw strength is untuned.

## Two-handed hold: stop the ball drifting toward the player

**Report.** The developer said the ball centres well between the controllers with a double grab, but also drifts back toward the rig camera. They asked for that axis to be left alone: centre across the hands, do not move the ball backward.

**Cause.** The two-handed carry target was the plain average of the two controller positions, which discards the `holdOffset` captured at the grab. The ball sits forward of the owning controller by that offset, so averaging the raw controller positions pulled the ball back by roughly half of it, toward the chest.

**Change.** `HandGrabber.KeepDepth` now slides the midpoint along the direction the player faces until it is as far out as the single-hand target. The hands still decide where the ball sits across and up; the ball keeps the reach it already had. The axis is the head's forward flattened onto the horizontal plane, so looking down at the ball does not tilt what "toward the player" means and shift the ball as the head moves. A `viewReference` field holds the head, assigned to the rig's `CenterEyeAnchor` on both grabbers and falling back to the main camera; without one the ball simply centres between the hands as before.

**Checks.** PlayMode **28/28**, scene validated clean. `TwoHandedHoldKeepsTheBallAtArmsLength` places a head at the origin looking down +Z, puts the ball at z 0.8 with both controllers behind it at z 0.7 and off to one side, and asserts after the blend that the ball centres to the average of the two hand x positions while staying at z 0.8 rather than being dragged back to the controllers.

**Limit.** Not yet felt on a headset. The ball still takes the owning hand's rotation rather than the axis between the hands.

## Grab pose: twisting moved the ball, and recall landed off-centre

**Report.** From a live Play session the developer reported that turning the controllers moved the ball, which it should not, and that the ball no longer came back centred. They worked out the second cause themselves: the recall stopped as soon as the ball touched one controller instead of travelling all the way to the middle of both.

**Watching the session.** A 10 Hz recorder was installed through `EditorApplication.update` to log hand poses, ball position and the hand midpoint while the ball was held. It confirmed live tracked hands and a loose ball. No script was edited until Play had stopped, since recompiling forces a domain reload and would have ended the session.

**Cause 1, twisting moved the ball.** The carried point was `transform.position + transform.rotation * holdOffset`, so the grab offset was rotated by the hand and the ball orbited the wrist at that radius. Caught at full stretch the offset is large, so a wrist turn swung the ball a long way. The two-handed case was worse: the depth correction added for the previous fix read its depth from that same rotated point, so every twist changed the correction and the ball wandered in and out instead of sitting still. That regression came from the earlier `KeepDepth` change, which made centring depend on rotation when it had not before.

**Cause 2, recall landed off-centre.** Both grips are held during the call gesture, so the first hand whose reach the incoming ball entered grabbed it. The flight ended there, off to one side, with a large hold offset, and the other hand was usually too far away to become support, leaving a lopsided one-handed hold.

**Change.** Held with two hands the ball now rides on where the hands *are*, never on how they are turned: the target is the midpoint of the two hand positions plus a depth captured once, when the second hand arrives, along the player's flattened facing direction. Rotation no longer enters the two-handed target at all. For a single hand the offset still rotates, which is what a ball in one hand really does, but `SettleHold` now eases it in to `GrabSettings.HoldDistance`, 0.11 m by default, over `HoldSettle`, 0.15 s, so a ball caught at arm's length draws in to the hand and a wrist turn no longer swings it far. Because a two-handed ball does not swing with the wrist, the lever-arm contribution to the throw is scaled by `1 - supportBlend` so it fades out as the second hand takes hold. For the recall, `Ball.InTransit` reserves a returning ball so `FindEligible` ignores it, and on arrival `BallRecall.Deliver` puts it into every hand still asking, through the new `HandGrabber.TryTakeDelivered`, which skips the reach test but still refuses a hand that is not holding its grip or is untracked. The first hand takes ownership and the second steadies it, so a called ball lands centred between the controllers. Nothing takes it if both grips were released, and it falls.

**Checks.** EditMode **9/9**, PlayMode **28/28**. `TwoHandedHoldKeepsTheBallAtArmsLength` now also turns both controllers to sharp angles after the hold has settled and asserts the ball moves less than 3 cm. `AReturningBallIsNotSnatchedPartWayIn` puts a squeezing hand directly on the flight path and asserts the delivery is not cut short. `RecallWithBothGripsHeldArrivesCentredInBothHands` asserts a called ball arrives held by both hands and within 10 cm of their midpoint. The recall tests now build two hands rather than one. The old `CatchingAReturningBallStopsTheFlight` test was removed: catching a returning ball early is exactly the behaviour that caused the off-centre hold, so it is now deliberately prevented.

**Limits.** None of this has been felt on a headset yet; the developer's next session is the real check. The ball still takes the owning hand's rotation rather than the axis between the hands. Issue 3 from the developer's list, that a throw follows only the last flick rather than the whole arm trajectory, is untouched.
